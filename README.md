# Fretmap

A guitar fretboard trainer that runs as a single page: intervals, scales and modes, positions, chord-aware drills, chord progressions to strum and arpeggiate with a metronome, and exercises you can gather into named practice sessions.

**Live:** https://ergooding.github.io/fretmap/

## Modes

- **Explore**: see every degree of a scale around the key. It draws the neck the way Drill does: the whole neck in a 7-fret window you scroll, a card beside the neck with the same What the neck shows key, and Show: Chord tones as solid chord tones over pearl passing tones. With a song in focus, it explains the song instead: its key and the pentatonic to solo with, what each chord does there, the notes and mode to play over it (with swaps for chords from outside the key), and how it leads into the next chord.
- **Quiz**: interval quizzes that weight the intervals you miss. **Name it** asks which degree a note is; **Find it** asks where a degree sits, with a How to find it panel showing the shape each interval makes on the neck.
- **Drill**: one builder, a dropdown per row down the side, sets up everything you play. On desktop each dropdown opens a menu where every choice has a tooltip that explains it in plain words, with an example in your key; the dropdown's own tooltip explains the current pick. The Chords dropdown also lists your songs, and the Strum dropdown every strum pattern.
  - **Chords**: **One chord** (pick which one in the **Over** row: I, ii, V and so on), a common progression (pop, doo-wop, ii V I, 12-bar blues, Andalusian and more) built from the key and mode, or one of your songs from the **Song ▾** menu. A song with parts adds a **Part** row to play the whole song or just one part. The **Add 7ths** switch on this row plays 4-note 7th chords instead of triads.
  - **Play each chord**: **Strum**, **Pick**, **Strum, then pick**, **Strum, then scale**, a **Scale line**, or **Chord tones**. Strum, then scale strums each chord for a bar, then runs up or down the scale for a bar, one step at a time, from the chord's root to a note of the next chord (its bass note when the steps allow), so the run leads into the change; the neck shows degrees during the run, with the next chord's fingers ringed. Strumming adds a **Strum** row: the common patterns, **Boom-chuck** (bass on 1 and 3, a strum on 2 and 4, walking into each new chord), and an **All 21 patterns ▾** menu with the rest and your own. Picking adds a **Pick** row: **Arpeggio**, **Crosspick** (rolls across the top three strings with strict alternate picking) or **Travis** (the thumb alternating the bass, fingers on the ands). Strum, then pick shows both rows, so any strum pairs with any pick. A scale line plays a **Line** pattern (up and down, two strings, 3rds, groups of 3 or 4) starting and ending on the root; over a progression, **Over the changes** either runs straight through the box while the chords go round underneath, or restarts the pattern from each chord's root. Chord tones play the notes of whichever chord is sounding, bar by bar: as an arpeggio, circling them from the scale notes above and below, or as a walk that lands a chord tone on every beat. Lines take a **Technique**: any picking, strict alternate, or hammer-ons and pull-offs, each marked in the tab. The strum card under the neck shows the pattern's steps (plain 8ths through ghost strums, 16ths, syncopation, and shuffle) with the counting under each; edit any step to make your own.
  - **Where**: **Follow the focus** (the position in the header), **Open position**, **Each shape in turn** (the next CAGED shape of the home chord after every pass), or the **Whole neck**, where lines climb through all five shapes and each chord takes the usual shape closest to the nut. Boom-chuck, Crosspick and Travis move to the open position and back.
  - **Save as a new exercise** keeps the setup under Yours in the Exercises tab.

  The neck, tab and song sheet follow along: a tile per bar with the chord, its roman numeral and the frets you'll play, lighting up the current bar. When chords are strummed or picked, the neck shows **Fingers**: each note of the shape carries the finger that frets it (1 index to 4 pinky) in its degree color, with × on strings you don't play, a hollow ring on open strings, a bar for a barre, and the rest of the chord faded. Dashed rings show where each finger goes for the next chord, a green ring marks a finger that stays, and a line above the strum card spells out the change. While playing, the rings appear in the last bar before the change (the whole pick bar, or the second half of a strummed bar). The tab's finger numbers match. Dot labels in ⚙ can switch back to Key, Chord or Notes. Tempo, Play, the count and Mark clean sit in one practice bar. Everything else (notes per beat, click, notes, shuffle, dot labels, what the neck shows while playing, and the tempo ramp) is in the ⚙ Settings sheet.
- **Exercises**: a library of **Starting points** (box patterns, chord tones, picking technique, the home chord in each CAGED shape, progressions, your songs), the setups you saved under **Yours**, and the interval quiz under **Ear**. Each one opens Drill with the same builder rows, set up for it, and is built from the focus (key, scale, mode, and position if you pick one) in the header above the tabs, which every tab shares; pentatonic and blues focuses borrow chords from the 7-note mode they sit in. Try any exercise on its own, or add it to a named **practice session**: an ordered list you can rename, reorder (drag or ↑ ↓), duplicate or delete, which remembers where you stopped. The app starts with a **Weeknight** session (box patterns, picking technique, progressions, your songs). Exercises in a session and in the library show what they start with as chips. An exercise shows a ✓ on each choice played clean at the goal tempo, and a strip to step through the session. **Customize** switches the same rows to editing in place: pick from a dropdown to set where the exercise starts, pin a key with the **Key** pill or a box from the **Where** dropdown, set the goal, then Save or Cancel. In Drill the neck plays what you pick while you edit; in a session's edit view it opens right in the list. Changes made while playing can also be saved back to the session.
- **Songs**: your songs, with their Songsterr links and a rating for how well you play them. Type each part's chords the way you'd write them (for example "(A, E6, Dmaj7) x2, A, E6, D, A"), a bar each, with slash chords such as C/G keeping their bass note lowest and square brackets sharing a bar ([C G] for two beats each, [C . . G] for three and one); parts play one after another. Tap a chord to pick its voicing from a few shapes drawn as diagrams, or type your own, such as 000220. A capo setting counts frets from the capo and plays everything that many half steps higher, so you type the shapes as the tab writes them. Each song keeps its own strum pattern, edited here or in Drill, and any part can have its own pattern instead. **Practice** opens Drill on the song, where the Part row plays the whole song or just one part.

Progress is kept in the browser; add a GitHub token with only the gist permission to sync it between devices through a private gist (under Sync and backup on the Exercises tab), or export and import it as a file. The focus bar has two modes: **Key** (key, scale, mode and position) and **Song**, which focuses every tab on one of your songs: its key everywhere, and its chords, strum pattern and tempo in Drill. Switching back to Key puts back the key you had. The ? button opens a glossary, along with the keyboard shortcuts. It all stays one page, but the browser's Back button steps back through the tabs, songs, settings and glossary you opened.

The layout switches between phone and desktop based on the window shape. The ◐ button in the header overrides it. On a phone the builder's dropdowns are the phone's own, and the builder starts folded so the neck shows on the first screen. The neck lies sideways in every layout, high e on top like the tab. On an upright phone a box fits the screen and the whole neck scrolls left and right. A whole-neck drill shows about 7 frets on any screen, the reach of one hand position, and the neck and the tab both slide along with the drill as it plays; you can scroll them yourself too. Beside the neck on desktop, one chord card shows the chord, its shape, and two folded explainers: What the neck shows (a key drawn with the neck's own dots) and How the chord is built. Chord diagrams, there and in a song's shape picker, are small sideways necks in the same style: high e on top, open strings as rings left of the nut, × for a string you skip, and the same degree colors and labels as the big neck.

## Project layout

- `index.html`: the whole app, with no build step. Edit it and push; GitHub Pages redeploys in about a minute.
- `tests/`: checks that run the app in a simulated browser (jsdom).
- `.nojekyll`: tells GitHub Pages to serve the files as-is.

## Running the tests

```
npm install
npm test
```

`npm run test:tips` and `npm run test:log` are quick. `npm run test:rhythm` checks every progression across keys, modes and boxes, and `npm run test:audit` tries every drill setting; each takes a few minutes.
